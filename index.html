📌 Overview

Structural Information Evolution (HTSIE) is an ongoing independent research programme investigating whether structural information can provide a useful framework for describing the evolution of complex systems across physical, mathematical, and computational scales.

The programme studies relationships among information, entropy, structure, geometry, dimensionality, and complexity.

Current research domains include condensed matter, quantum plasma, compact stars, black-hole systems, computational systems, neural-network representations, sparse autoencoders, and large language models.

Research status: HTSIE is an open and exploratory research programme. Individual mathematical constructions, computational results, and physical interpretations are treated as separate research components and should be evaluated according to their own evidence.

🔬 Current Research Programme

Structural Information Evolution

Mathematical and conceptual descriptions of how structural information may change across scales and dynamical regimes.

HTSIE-SAE

Application of structural-information concepts to sparse autoencoders, learned representations, activation covariance, and representation geometry.

Knowledge Entropy

Investigation of entropy-like quantities intended to characterize the organization, concentration, and evolution of structured information.

Spectral Dimension

Investigation of spectral observables, spectral dimension flow, and effective dimensionality as possible indicators of structural change across scales.

Cross-System Analysis

Comparative investigation of structural signatures across physically and computationally different systems.

Computational Physics

Numerical experiments, PyTorch implementations, statistical analysis, reproducibility checks, and falsification-oriented testing.

🧭 Core Research Hypothesis

A recurring research direction within HTSIE investigates whether changes in structural constraints may be related to changes in effective degrees of freedom and accessible state space. 

Structural Constraints
        ↓
Effective Degrees of Freedom
        ↓
Accessible State Space
        ↓
Information Representation
        ↓
Structural Information Evolution

A more specific working hypothesis is:

Constraint Increase → Effective Freedom Reduction → Accessible State-Space Reduction → Information-Dimension Change

This relationship is treated as a research hypothesis, not as an established universal law.
