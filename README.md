# HTSIE Research Program

**Structural Information Evolution (HTSIE)** is an ongoing research programme investigating whether common structural information principles can describe the evolution of complex systems across physical, mathematical, and computational scales.

The programme explores how **information, entropy, structure, geometry, and complexity** evolve across systems ranging from condensed matter and compact stars to black holes, sparse autoencoders, and large language models.

The central research question is:

> **Can structural information provide a common language for understanding the emergence and evolution of organized complexity across very different physical and computational systems?**

---

## 🔬 Research Programme

The HTSIE programme currently develops several interconnected research directions:

### 1. Structural Information Evolution

Development of a general framework for describing how structural information changes across scales and dynamical regimes.

Key concepts include:

- Structural Information
- Information Evolution
- Entropy and Knowledge Structure
- Complexity
- Emergence
- Scale-dependent organization

### 2. HTSIE-SAE

Application of structural-information methods to **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)** and representation learning.

Current interests include:

- SAE feature structure
- Activation covariance
- Representation geometry
- Information concentration
- Feature emergence
- Interpretability of learned representations

### 3. Knowledge Entropy

Investigation of entropy-like quantities designed to characterize the organization, concentration, and evolution of structured information.

### 4. Cross-Scale Physical Systems

Comparative investigation of structural information across systems with very different physical scales, including:

- Condensed matter
- Quantum plasma
- Compact stars
- Black holes
- Complex computational systems
- Large language models

The objective is not to assume that these systems share identical dynamics, but to test whether **common structural information signatures** can be identified across otherwise very different regimes.

# 🧪 Research Methodology

The HTSIE programme combines several complementary approaches:

### Mathematical Analysis

Development of explicit definitions, structural quantities, scaling relations, and theoretical propositions.

### Computational Experiments

Large-scale numerical experiments are used to identify patterns, test hypotheses, and investigate parameter regimes that are difficult to study analytically.

### Machine Learning Analysis

PyTorch-based experiments are used to study learned representations, particularly sparse representations and activation geometry in neural networks.

### Cross-System Comparison

The same structural quantities are examined across physically and computationally different systems to determine whether apparently universal patterns are robust or merely model-specific.

### Reproducibility

Computational observations are treated as evidence for hypothesis generation and validation, while theoretical claims are kept distinct from empirical or numerical observations.
