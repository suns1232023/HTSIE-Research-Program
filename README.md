# HTSIE Research Programme

**Structural Information Evolution (HTSIE)** is an ongoing independent research programme investigating whether structural information can provide a useful framework for describing the evolution of complex systems across physical, mathematical, and computational scales.

The programme studies relationships among **information, entropy, structure, geometry, dimensionality, and complexity** in systems ranging from condensed matter and compact objects to computational representations, sparse autoencoders, and large language models.

> **Central research question:**
> Can structural information provide a common language for studying the emergence and evolution of organized complexity across otherwise different physical and computational systems?

The programme is exploratory and research-oriented. Individual mathematical constructions, computational results, and physical interpretations are treated as separate research components and should be evaluated according to their own evidence.

---

## Research Programme

HTSIE currently develops several interconnected research directions.

### 1. Structural Information Evolution

Development of mathematical and conceptual descriptions of how structural information may change across scales and dynamical regimes.

Current concepts include:

* Structural information
* Information evolution
* Entropy and information organization
* Complexity
* Emergence
* Scale-dependent structure
* Information concentration
* Effective dimensionality

### 2. HTSIE-SAE

Application of structural-information concepts to **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)** and learned representations.

Current research includes:

* SAE feature structure
* Activation covariance
* Representation geometry
* Information concentration
* Feature emergence
* Effective dimensionality
* Interpretability of learned representations

### 3. Knowledge Entropy

Investigation of entropy-like quantities intended to characterize the organization, concentration, and evolution of structured information.

The objective is to examine whether such quantities can be defined mathematically and tested computationally rather than assumed to possess universal significance in advance.

### 4. Spectral and Effective-Dimension Analysis

Investigation of spectral observables and effective dimensionality as possible indicators of structural change across scales.

This direction includes:

* Spectral dimension
* Dimension flow
* Effective dimensionality
* Spectral structure
* Scale-dependent geometric behaviour
* Numerical spectral analysis

### 5. Cross-System Structural Comparison

Comparative investigation of structural information across systems with substantially different physical or computational characteristics, including:

* Condensed matter
* Quantum plasma
* Compact stars
* Black-hole systems
* Computational systems
* Neural-network representations
* Large language models

The purpose is not to assume that these systems obey identical dynamics. Instead, the research asks whether particular structural signatures can be identified, formalized, and independently tested across different regimes.

# Research Methodology

## Mathematical Analysis

The programme develops explicit definitions, mathematical quantities, scaling relations, structural constraints, and theoretical propositions.

Where possible, qualitative concepts are translated into mathematical objects that can be examined independently.

## Computational Experiments

Numerical experiments are used to:

* investigate proposed relationships;
* explore parameter regimes;
* identify possible structural patterns;
* test mathematical expectations;
* search for counterexamples;
* evaluate numerical stability.

Computational results are treated as observations requiring reproducibility and appropriate interpretation.

## Machine Learning Analysis

PyTorch-based experiments investigate learned representations, particularly sparse representations and activation geometry in neural networks.

The current focus includes covariance structure, feature organization, activation distributions, and effective dimensionality.

## Cross-System Comparison

The programme compares structural quantities across physically and computationally different systems.

Such comparisons are treated as hypotheses to be tested rather than evidence that the underlying systems necessarily share the same physical mechanism.

## Reproducibility

Reproducibility is treated as an explicit part of the research process.

Where practical, the repository provides:

* source code;
* computational procedures;
* parameter definitions;
* numerical outputs;
* datasets or dataset links;
* version information;
* archival records.
